Strategic Blueprint for Advancing Kinematic Inference: Pushing Mean Relative Accuracy Beyond 0.85 in the QuantiPhy Challenge
The QuantiPhy Challenge represents a defining inflection point in the evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), transitioning the paradigm from qualitative visual description to rigorous, numerically faithful physical reasoning. Operating across microscopic, macroscopic, and astronomical domains, the benchmark demands that models accurately infer unobservable kinematic properties—such as velocity and acceleration—based on a single provided physical prior and raw video input. The difficulty of this task is evidenced by the performance of state-of-the-art frontier models; as of late 2025, human annotators achieve a Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) of 55.6, whereas leading proprietary models like ChatGPT-5.1 and Gemini-2.5 Pro manage only 48.4 and 46.4, respectively. Open-weight models trail further behind, with the 32B parameter Qwen3-VL achieving an MRA of 40.2.   

Against this backdrop, the current competitive pipeline has achieved a highly commendable MRA of 0.811 across the four diagnostic categories (S2, D2, S3, D3), utilizing a sophisticated ensemble of Claude Opus reasoning, geometric solving, and model-free post-rules. However, ascending to the first-place leaderboard threshold of 0.8525 requires a precise, systematic extraction of an additional +0.0415 overall MRA.   

A rigorous analysis of the diagnostic telemetry reveals that the performance deficits are deeply structured. The most substantial vectors for improvement lie within the 3D Static (S3) non-lab settings (currently at 0.742), S3 lab settings (0.782), and S2 Internet datasets (0.796). The current architecture relies heavily on direct numerical outputs from Claude Opus for complex 3D environments, leaving the system dangerously exposed to the well-documented spatial hallucinations inherent in autoregressive token generation. Furthermore, the routing architecture exhibits a hard performance ceiling, and the legacy geometric solvers have proven brittle when fed noisy focal length estimations.   

This exhaustive report details a comprehensive, theoretically grounded, and computationally viable suite of interventions designed strictly around the testing inputs. By migrating from legacy generative approaches to deterministic Program-of-Thought (PoT) execution, replacing unstable Field of View (FOV) estimators with state-of-the-art metric foundation models like Apple's Depth Pro, and reformulating the trajectory optimization paradigm to explicitly align with the mathematical realities of the MRA metric, the +0.04 deficit can be systematically neutralized.
1. Critical Evaluation of the Baseline Architecture and Underlying Fallacies
To engineer novel solutions, it is first necessary to deconstruct the empirical failures noted in the current pipeline telemetry. Understanding why certain high-effort interventions failed prevents the duplication of misaligned strategies and highlights the fundamental theoretical boundaries of the current system.
1.1 The Mathematical Limitations of Autoregressive Generative Kinematics
The current routing logic defaults to directing 3D non-lab questions (simulation and segmented subsets) to Claude Opus for a direct numeric answer, utilizing the median of three independent inference runs to stabilize variance. The pipeline documentation notes that attempting to saturate the runs up to seven yielded a negligible gain of +0.005.   

This saturation behavior is highly indicative of epistemic uncertainty rather than aleatoric noise. Vision-Language Models, regardless of their parameter count or training corpus, are fundamentally autoregressive sequence predictors. When prompted to deduce depth orderings, relative distances, and explicit physical integrations over time directly within their latent space, they frequently produce confident but mathematically incorrect outputs. The ceiling for direct answering is inherently capped because the VLM is attempting to execute floating-point trigonometric projections and kinematic calculus without an external scratchpad. The variance observed across the three Claude runs is a symptom of the model's inability to maintain numerical precision across extended reasoning chains, not a lack of initial visual feature extraction. Attempts to bypass this by utilizing GPT-6 Astra or Gemini 3.1 Pro resulted in similar or worse performance (e.g., Gemini dropping to 0.565 on validation), confirming that direct numerical regression from video tokens is a structural dead end for exact kinematic inference.

1.3 The Camera Parallax Misconception in Internet Subsets
Within the S2 Internet subset, previous attempts at camera-motion compensation were abandoned because "34 of 36 tracks change < 5%". This observation suggests that the compensation algorithm likely relied on 2D homographies or affine matrix transformations to stabilize background pixels. However, the internet videos in the dataset represent "in-the-wild" recordings, which frequently feature translatory camera ego-motion.   

A pure 2D homography cannot correctly model or compensate for 3D translation without violating epipolar geometric constraints, as it assumes the scene is perfectly planar or the camera is undergoing pure rotation. The lack of change in the tracks indicates that the compensation algorithm failed to properly model the fundamental matrix of the scene, rendering the conclusion—that camera motion compensation is ineffective—theoretically unsound. Correcting this requires dense metric point clouds rather than planar pixel warping.
2. Revolutionizing 3D Geometry via Zero-Shot Metric Foundation Models
The existing reliance on generic depth estimators or early-generation foundation models for FOV and subsequent geometry solving is a primary contributor to the 0.742 bottleneck in the S3 non-lab subset. While the pipeline mentions MoGe-2, this model has documented vulnerabilities. Although MoGe-2 is a powerful generative framework that enforces metric scale and bypasses the focal-distance ambiguity via point map representation, it struggles with open-domain scale variations (e.g., distinguishing vast outdoor landscapes from tight indoor lab setups) and requires complex mismatch filtering when transitioning from synthetic to real-world datasets.   

To systematically eradicate the S3 deficit, the pipeline must pivot to a foundation model explicitly engineered for unconstrained absolute metric scale and independent, zero-shot focal length estimation.
2.1 The Architectural Superiority of Apple Depth Pro
Released in late 2024, Apple's Depth Pro represents a paradigm shift in monocular metric depth estimation, synthesizing high-resolution metric depth maps with unparalleled sharpness. Unlike Metric3D v2, which occasionally requires dataset-specific intrinsic distributions or prior camera knowledge to achieve optimal scale, Depth Pro produces 2.25-megapixel depth maps natively in 0.3 seconds.   

The architectural innovations of Depth Pro render it uniquely suited for the QuantiPhy benchmark constraints:

- Multi-Scale Vision Transformer: To bypass the quadratic computational complexity of processing megapixels in a standard Vision Transformer, Depth Pro utilizes a multi-scale ViT architecture. The input image is resized to a fixed resolution of 1536×15361536 \times 15361536×1536, then downsampled to 768×768768 \times 768768×768 and 384×384384 \times 384384×384. These scales are split into overlapping patches of 384×384384 \times 384384×384 and processed through a shared patch encoder built on a DINOv2 ViT-L backbone. This allows the model to capture both global scene context (essential for accurate scale) and fine-grained, high-frequency details (essential for precise object bounding boundaries).   

- Independent FOV Network: Most critically for QuantiPhy, Depth Pro does not couple its focal length estimation to its depth prediction. It features a dedicated Field-of-View head. This small convolutional network ingests frozen features from the primary depth network alongside task-specific features from a separate ViT image encoder to predict the horizontal angular field-of-view in degrees. By separating these objectives during training, Depth Pro leverages massive image datasets that possess focal length metadata but lack depth supervision, allowing it to generalize zero-shot to entirely unseen camera intrinsics.   

- Boundary Tracing Fidelity: QuantiPhy relies heavily on pixel tracking for size and velocity priors. Standard depth models suffer from "flying pixels" and blurry occlusion boundaries, which corrupt the depth reading of a targeted object. Depth Pro utilizes a specialized training curriculum combining real and synthetic datasets with a loss function utilizing first- and second-order multi-scale derivatives to harden boundary predictions. It achieves a state-of-the-art boundary F1 score of 0.409 on the Sintel dataset, compared to 0.321 for prior leading models.

3. Eradicating Spatial Hallucination via Programmatic Kinematic Reasoning (Program-of-Thought)
Even with perfect metric geometry recovered by foundation models, the pipeline still faces a critical bottleneck: synthesizing the geometric data into a final answer for velocity or acceleration. The reliance on Claude Opus to return a "direct numeric answer" based on 16 uniform frames is fundamentally flawed. While Claude possesses exceptional code-generation and symbolic logic capabilities, its ability to perform explicit mathematical integration over time using floating-point numbers is highly unreliable.   

To capture the elusive +.04 MRA, particularly in the D3 dynamic scenarios and the S2 Internet subsets, the pipeline must entirely strip the mathematical burden from the VLM and transition to a Program-of-Thought (PoT) architecture.

For the dynamic motion categories (D2 and D3), which account for a massive portion of the scored questions (1,137 and 933 items respectively), the pipeline currently extracts kinematic measurements using "robust local polynomial fits" where speed at a specific time is derived from the derivative of a ±0.5\pm 0.5±0.5 second quadratic fit.   

While computationally lightweight, utilizing a localized quadratic fit over a narrow 1.0-second temporal window introduces severe vulnerability. Pixel tracking data derived from bounding boxes is inherently subjected to high-frequency quantization noise (pixel jitter). A localized quadratic curve ignores the global kinematic context of the entire clip, meaning a slight bounding box tremor directly at the target timestamp will radically alter the calculated derivative.

This eliminates the halo noise propagation entirely.
7. Refutation of Pre-existing Pipeline Assumptions
