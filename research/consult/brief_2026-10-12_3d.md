# QuantiPhy Challenge (NeurIPS 2026), Track A: two 3D problems we cannot crack. Ideas wanted.

Please be concrete, quantitative and critical. We want methods we have NOT tried, ranked by expected gain per
effort, each with a cheap way to check it before spending a leaderboard upload (5 per day). Cite papers/code
when you know them.

## Task in one paragraph

Each question: one short video (1-10 s, 24 fps), a "prior" (one exactly-true quantity of one object, e.g.
"height of the forklift = 2.47m", "speed of the red blood cell = 4.78 m/s", "acceleration of the white car =
4m/s^2", "Gravity acc = 9.8m/s^2"), and for 3D videos a `depth_info` text with object-to-camera distances at
given times, e.g. `t=1.0s, distance_forklift_camera = 16.80 m / t=2.0s, distance_forklift_camera = 19.10 m`
(usually 1-2 objects x 2 times; sometimes only the prior object, sometimes only others). The question asks
another quantity: a size, a distance between objects, a speed at time t, an acceleration at t.
Metric: Mean Relative Accuracy, item score = fraction of thresholds th in {0.1..0.9, 0.95} with
|pred-gt|/gt < 1-th (<5% error = 1.0, 5-10% = 0.9, 10-20% = 0.8 ... >=90% = 0). Categories S2/D2/S3/D3
(S = size prior, D = motion prior; 2 = planar 2D video, 3 = 3D) are averaged equally.

## Where we are

Leaderboard: we are at ~.81 (S2 .815, D2 .844, S3 .762, D3 .797); #1 is .8525. The 3D categories are the gap.
Test composition: S3 = 334 lab + 169 simulation + 75 "segmented" (pixel-aligned copies of a simulation clip on
white background); D3 = 457 lab + 425 simulation + 87 segmented.
Pipeline: Claude Opus (vision LLM) sees 16-32 labelled frames, returns object tracks (points/boxes per frame)
and its own direct numeric answers; a pinhole geometry solver uses tracks + prior + depth_info; post-rules use
"scene families" (several clips of the same scene: their stated priors answer each other's questions exactly,
a per-clip scale error of the LLM's answers is corrected from family facts, answers about one object are pooled).

## Problem 1: 3D simulation clips (Blender-like renders), camera intrinsics unknown

- The camera's horizontal FOV varies from 8 to 103 deg (modes 15-20 and 35-45), and differs between cameras of
  the same scene. The camera may move (not verified). No EXIF/metadata.
- Our geometry: pinhole, principal point at centre, unknown f solved so the prior computed in 3D equals its
  stated value (depth from depth_info, interpolated in time; objects without depth get an estimated range;
  looming from box size); then target = pixels x range / f. Sizes are treated fronto-parallel.
- Result: geometry is much WORSE than the LLM's direct guesses on 3D non-lab: per changed answer -0.19 on sizes,
  -0.09 (S3) / -0.13 (D3) on motion targets. So we use the LLM's direct answers (median of 3 runs; 1 run .783,
  3 runs .799, 7 runs .804 on validation, so more runs saturate).
- Scene families help a lot where they exist: on 71 D3 stated facts, leave-one-fact-out raw .790 -> per-clip
  scale correction .855 -> + pooling .908 -> both .951. But families cover only part of the set.
- Suspected reasons geometry fails: f poorly conditioned when the prior moves along the line of sight or its
  depth is missing; object extents in pixels are foreshortened (cars/eagles at oblique angles, "length" along
  the view direction); depth_info may be range to the object's origin/centre, not to the visible surface;
  tracks from an LLM are coarse (a few px to tens of px error on small objects); speed priors need a full 3D
  velocity while only 2 depth samples are given.
- Questions look like: "length of the trolley", "eagle's wingspan", "diameter of the bicycle wheel",
  "speed of the blue car at 1s", "acceleration of the car at 1s", "height of the turning yellow car".

What we want: a way to recover intrinsics/scale on these renders, or a better hybrid of geometry and the LLM's
priors (e.g. when to trust which), or monocular metric-depth / single-view metrology / known-object-size priors
/ vanishing points / ground-plane methods that work here; and how to combine them under MRA.

## Problem 2: lab clips (real camera, fixed), labels are noisy and biased

- Fixed camera: f ~ 722 px at 1280 px width (we verified), captured at 25 fps, converted to 24 fps by dropping
  frames. depth_info gives distances (from a ToF depth camera).
- Labels (paper arXiv:2512.19526, App. D): an annotator hand-clicks the object centre on frames, reads the depth
  at that pixel from the ToF map, back-projects with the fixed camera, and takes FORWARD differences of
  consecutive 25 fps frames: speed = |P(k+1) - P(k)| x 25; path length = sum of segments.
- Consequence: click noise (~2 px) + depth noise (cm) adds a positive bias to speed labels, large for slow or
  small objects (|noise| / (v/25) is not small), and inflates path lengths. Our "label-process emulator" (Monte
  Carlo of that procedure, noise fitted on the stated priors; predicted MRA-optimal factor per row) gave
  S3 +0.015 on the leaderboard; D3 lab motion gave ~0 (uniform x1.40 also 0). On validation lab speed labels
  run ~1.36x our physical estimates on a few scenes.
- Sizes of lab objects: measured with f + depth come out 0.94x stated (motion blur); props reuse across events
  (balls, books, cups, toys), we pool them.
- S3 lab motion subset scores ~.69, D3 lab motion ~.67 (item means); static targets ~.75.

What we want: a better model of the label process (e.g. was depth read at the clicked pixel with holes/edges
of the ToF map, so balls read too far?; is the forward difference at frame k or k-1 for "speed at t"?; is the
depth mean over a window?), a principled per-row correction, or a way to estimate per-scene label bias from the
inputs alone (we may not use test labels). Also how to measure lab motion better from the video (24 fps,
motion blur, falling/rolling/sliding toys, balls, books on ramps, carts).

## What we already tried that did not help
More LLM runs beyond 3; GPT and Gemini as extra voters (no ensemble gain, answers highly correlated);
higher reasoning effort; an LLM agent loop with measuring tools; zoomed re-measurement of extents; dense
optical-flow / GrabCut tracking; camera-motion compensation; MRA "shading" (shrinking answers); emulating a
Blender backward-difference label convention; the lab emulator on D3; uniform lab speed factors.
