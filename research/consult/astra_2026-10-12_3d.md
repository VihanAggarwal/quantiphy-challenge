# Problem 1: simulations

## Diagnosis and ceiling

### The focal fit can be intrinsically ill-conditioned

For a fixed camera, axial depth \(z\), centered image coordinate \(a=u-c_x\), and similarly \(b\),
\[
V^2=\dot z^2+
\frac{(z\dot a+\dot z a)^2+(z\dot b+\dot z b)^2}{f^2}.
\]

Consequently,
\[
f^2=\frac{A}{V^2-\dot z^2}.
\]

If only 10% of squared velocity is transverse, then approximately **5% uncertainty in the speed prior creates 50% uncertainty in fitted focal length**, even before depth and track errors. A numerically successful root solve is not evidence that \(f\) has been recovered.

### A dimension prior is not necessarily a focal calibration

For an approximately linear object dimension \(L\), viewed at angle \(\alpha\) to the viewing direction,
\[
\ell \approx \frac{f L\sin\alpha}{z}.
\]
Without orientation, the prior identifies \(f\sin\alpha\), not \(f\). Calibrating on a foreshortened trolley length then applying the inferred \(f\) to another object can be catastrophically wrong.

### Realistic ceiling

Some rows are simply not metrically identifiable from the available anchors. You can still answer them statistically, but a reconstruction system cannot make them exact.

I would aim for **+.04–.08 on selected, reasonably covered simulation subsets**, rather than a universal .95 reconstruction solution. Your .951 family result is encouraging for redundant facts, but it is not evidence that isolated clips have the same attainable accuracy.

## 1. Audit distance semantics and identifiability

This is more than changing a constant in the solver.

### A. Explicitly support two meanings of `depth_info`

Let
\[
q=((u-c_x)/f,(v-c_y)/f,1).
\]

- **Axial depth:** \(P=zq\).
- **Euclidean camera range:** \(P=rq/\|q\|\).

Confusing range with axial depth introduces a factor \(\sec\theta\). At the horizontal edge:

- 20° horizontal FOV: approximately **1.5%**;
- 40°: approximately **6.4%**;
- 103°: approximately **61%**.

The error can partially cancel between prior and target, but only when their viewing angles are similar. Do not assume lab and Blender use the same convention.

Also distinguish:

- object origin;
- geometric center;
- visible front surface;
- clicked surface point.

These are not interchangeable observations.

### B. Replace “a focal solution exists” with a profile

Evaluate the prior residual over a focal/FOV grid, rather than just finding a root. Add plausible uncertainty to tracks and depths. Record:

- width of the compatible focal interval;
- whether the optimum hits a boundary;
- sensitivity to dropping a depth observation;
- sensitivity to a few-pixel perturbation;
- target-answer spread, not just focal spread.

Some targets are stable even when focal length is not; others are the reverse.

**Cheap validation:** Run this on 15–20 diverse clips and all usable independent stated-fact queries. Plot geometry’s error against predicted answer spread. If uncertainty does not rank failures, do not build a sophisticated fusion model on top.

**Deliverable:** geometry becomes a candidate with an uncertainty interval and an explicit failure flag. Never return a confidently calibrated \(f\) from a nearly radial prior.

## 2. Optimize MRA over a conditional predictive distribution

This is different from uniform “shading.”

For positive ground truth \(Y\), the MRA-optimal action is
\[
a^*=\arg\max_a \frac1{10}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{.05,.1,.2,\ldots,.9\}}
P\left(\frac{a}{1+\epsilon}<Y<\frac{a}{1-\epsilon}\mid x\right).
\]

Given weighted samples \(y_j\), this is easy: each sample contributes utility on intervals
\[
a\in((1-\epsilon)y_j,(1+\epsilon)y_j).
\]
Sweep interval endpoints or evaluate a dense one-dimensional grid.

### What to model

Use a mixture of:

- current three-run LLM estimate;
- geometric estimate;
- family/prop estimate, when independently available.

Estimate their **residual distributions on held-out videos**. The dispersion among three LLM runs is not a calibrated ground-truth posterior.

Keep the gate tiny. Useful variables are:

1. prior’s transverse-motion fraction;
2. whether target depth is actually observed;
3. geometric answer sensitivity;
4. rigid/fronto-parallel versus foreshortening-sensitive dimension;
5. consistency across independently measured frames;
6. object pixel size.

Start with perhaps three regimes: **trusted geometry / ambiguous / reject geometry**. Do not train a flexible meta-model on 24 videos.

**Cheap validation:** Cross-fitted predictions must beat both your unchanged baseline and a simple hand-written gate. Report gain on rows where the two estimators disagree strongly.

**Why promising:** Your average geometric changes are bad, but that does not imply every geometric change is bad. Selectivity can capture a small reliable subset without paying for the majority.

## 4. Estimate focal length independently of the quantity prior

Try a small frozen stack, not a full reconstruction project:

- **GeoCalib** — [cvg/GeoCalib](https://github.com/cvg/GeoCalib), *Learning-based Camera Calibration*, ECCV 2024.
- **MoGe** — [microsoft/MoGe](https://github.com/microsoft/MoGe), returns camera/geometry estimates.
- **Depth Pro** — [apple/ml-depth-pro](https://github.com/apple/ml-depth-pro), includes focal-length estimation.

Use 3–5 **original-background** frames per camera. Segmented-on-white copies are poor inputs for scene calibration; transfer camera estimates from the aligned original instead.

### Important implementation checks

- Convert predicted intrinsics back through resize/crop/letterbox transformations.
- Estimate one camera per clip unless there is evidence of zoom.
- Treat network agreement as weak evidence: their priors can be similarly wrong.
- Expect the 8–20° telephoto cases to be especially difficult. Ordinary-image calibration networks are not guaranteed to cover them.

### Give the networks an independent check

Where genuine orthogonal directions are visible, two vanishing points satisfy
\[
f^2=-(v_1-c)^\top(v_2-c)
\]
for centered principal point and square pixels. Use road markings, building edges, or clearly orthogonal rigid edges—not arbitrary converging lines.

Do this manually on a few pilot frames before engineering automatic line clustering. The reference is Criminisi, Reid and Zisserman, **“Single View Metrology,” IJCV 2000**.

Otherwise test candidates against withheld depth anchors or an independent size/motion fact. Do **not** judge them by how well the same prior can be fit after rescaling.

**Cheap validation:** Twenty clips, a few frames each; compare downstream target accuracy with each frozen focal estimate. A focal network need not recover perfect intrinsics to outperform a singular prior-based fit.

**Kill criterion:** temporal consistency without held-out target improvement is not enough.

## 5. Fit image tracks and sparse depths jointly

Your present “interpolate depth, then solve geometry” architecture can manufacture incorrect velocities and destroy acceleration.

Instead fit
\[
\min_{f,\theta}\ 
\sum_i \rho\!\left(\|\pi_f(P_\theta(t_i))-p_i\|^2/\sigma_p^2\right)
+\sum_j\rho\!\left((h(P_\theta(t_j))-d_j)^2/\sigma_d^2\right)
+\text{prior residual}.
\]

Here:

- \(h(P)=P_z\) or \(\|P\|\), according to method 1;
- \(\theta\) parameterizes a low-dimensional trajectory;
- \(f\) has a broad likelihood/prior from method 4, not a forced exact value.

Try only a few models:

- constant 3D velocity;
- constant acceleration over a short window;
- straight-line motion constrained to a ground plane;
- piecewise motion separated at visible turns or impacts.

Do not use constant acceleration over an entire clip with collisions.

### Useful invariant

For a fixed camera and Euclidean range,
\[
\|v\|^2=\dot r^2+r^2\|\dot{\hat q}\|^2.
\]
This exposes when radial observations already account for nearly all speed.

However, two widely separated ranges bound **average path speed**, not necessarily instantaneous speed at the question time. Avoid imposing an invalid instantaneous bound.

### Cheap validation: mask an anchor

On examples with two depth entries, fit while withholding one and predict it. Also withhold track intervals.

This gives a free test of the trajectory assumptions before consulting target labels. A method that cannot predict the second depth is unlikely to estimate acceleration correctly.

Use robust multi-point tracks from **CoTracker3**, [facebookresearch/co-tracker](https://github.com/facebookresearch/co-tracker), initialized with **SAM 2**, [facebookresearch/sam2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2). The new ingredient is **rigid/physical model fitting and uncertainty**, not “another optical-flow tracker.”

I would not start with VGGT/MASt3R here: dynamic objects and stationary or nearly stationary cameras undermine the usual static-scene multi-view advantage.

## 6. Fit a few object shapes and poses, not fronto-parallel boxes

Select classes with a defensible geometric model:

- wheel: circle in a plane;
- ball: sphere;
- book, box, cart body: cuboid or thin rectangle;
- vehicle: a small set of repeatable rigid landmarks and dimensions.

Fit pose and shared dimensions across several frames using calibrated projection, silhouettes, and available range observations. For repeated props, share **shape parameters**, not merely final scalar answers.

Examples:

- A wheel ellipse provides plane-orientation information. After perspective pose fitting, its diameter is substantially better defined than an arbitrary image-box width.
- A turning cuboid provides multiple orientations that constrain length/width/height jointly.
- Vehicle “height” should be extent along the object/world vertical axis, not image-box height, which can contain projected length.

Do **not** start with an eagle’s wingspan: articulation, incomplete wing extension, and semantic definition make it a much harder class.

**Cheap validation:** Fit on half the poses, predict silhouettes/keypoints in the other half. Then evaluate independent size facts. Forward projection should visibly explain the object before you trust its hidden dimension.

**Critical caveat:** standard object-size priors are dangerous for synthetic scenes. A rendered bicycle need not have a normal human-scale wheel. Use shape ratios/pose evidence, with metric scale anchored by the supplied facts.

# Problem 2: lab clips

## Diagnosis and ceiling

Your label-noise diagnosis is credible, but the magnitude depends strongly on **direction and temporal correlation**.

At \(z=2\) m and \(f=722\) px:

- 2 px position noise corresponds to approximately **5.5 mm** laterally;
- independent frame noise gives approximately **0.196 m/s** standard deviation per lateral velocity coordinate;
- 1 cm axial noise gives approximately **0.354 m/s** axial velocity standard deviation.

Those are large for slow toys.

But with adjacent-frame correlation \(\rho\), the velocity-noise scale is multiplied by \(\sqrt{1-\rho}\). Assuming independent ToF samples when \(\rho=.8\) overstates that component by a factor of about **2.24**.

Even perfect physical trajectories cannot recover independent annotation jitter. The honest ceiling is therefore the **Bayes-optimal MRA under a validated label distribution**, which you can estimate by posterior-predictive simulation. It is not 1.0. For perspective, in the idealized zero-motion, isotropic-Gaussian-noise case, speed follows a Maxwell distribution and its best deterministic MRA is only around **.60**.

I would seek **+.03–.06 on recoverable lab-motion subsets**, with lower expectations for slow, depth-dominated motion and possibly raw finite-difference acceleration.

## 3. Fit a trajectory on the source clock, then apply the exact label operation

This is my highest-priority lab experiment.

### A. Model dropped frames

A simple candidate source-frame map is
\[
k(n)=\lfloor 25n/24+\phi\rfloor,
\]
with the appropriate offset and drop phase. Actual conversion may use a different schedule, so test this hypothesis against motion.

A 24 fps output pair can span either 40 or 80 ms of source time. A naïve per-output-frame derivative can therefore show roughly doubled displacement near a dropped frame. A smooth fit on output timestamps largely averages this away, but not necessarily near impacts or queried instants.

Infer the phase by finding which map best explains smooth multi-point trajectories. Do not assume output frame \(n\) corresponds to source frame \(25n/24\) exactly.

### B. Fit the physical signal

Use robust multi-point or silhouette measurements, then fit short, event-separated models:

- ballistic arc for free flight;
- one-dimensional quadratic motion for a ramp;
- local cubic/smoothing spline where physics is unclear;
- separate segments before and after collision or release.

Gravity is a useful lab hypothesis, but do not impose 9.81 on arbitrary simulations when the supplied prior allows another value.

### C. Evaluate the label functional, not instantaneous velocity

If the question selects source frame \(k\), predict
\[
s_k=25\|P(t_k+.04)-P(t_k)\|,
\]
before adding label noise.

For constant acceleration, this corresponds approximately to velocity at \(t_k+.02\). In free fall that shift is **0.196 m/s**—nearly 20% when speed is 1 m/s.

Test a **small predefined set** of frame-selection conventions: nearest/floor source frame and forward versus preceding segment. Select a convention by held-out scenes, not separately by each validation label.

For acceleration, first establish whether labels use:

- vector second difference;
- difference of speed magnitudes;
- something smoothed.

These are physically different targets.

**Cheap validation:** On 6–8 clips with appreciable acceleration, compare:

1. current measurement;
2. smooth trajectory with current timing;
3. source-clock fit plus forward functional.

This separates tracking gains from convention gains. On held-out image frames, require better reprojection as well as better labels.

## 7. Make the label model conditional, anisotropic, and hierarchical

I would only pursue this after method 3, because otherwise timing error gets misidentified as noise.

### A. Model clicked-pixel depth, not independent position perturbations

For axial depth,
\[
P(u,v)=z(u,v)\,q(u,v).
\]

A click perturbation changes both ray direction and depth:
\[
J_{\rm click}
=\frac{z}{f}
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}
+q\,(\nabla z)^\top.
\]

This means two pixels on a ball’s flank can create substantially more 3D error than two pixels on a flat book. Add a separate low-probability foreground/background or invalid-depth component.

But you do not possess the original ToF map. **RGB-derived depth cannot reveal actual ToF holes.** Treat edge contamination as a latent hypothesis with a few pooled parameters, not something measured exactly from monocular depth.

### B. Direction matters

For small zero-mean velocity noise with covariance \(\Sigma\),
\[
E\|v+\epsilon\|
\approx \|v\|+
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma-\hat v^\top\Sigma\hat v}
{2\|v\|}.
\]

Thus axial noise causes different norm inflation for radial and transverse motion. A universal 1.36 or 1.40 multiplier cannot capture this.

### C. Condition on the stated prior as a benchmark observation

For a D3 lab row, infer
\[
p(\text{trajectory},\text{noise parameters}\mid
\text{video},\text{depth info},\text{stated label}),
\]
then predict the target label.

Do not first treat a noisy speed prior as exact physical speed and subsequently add a label-inflation correction. That can double-count the noise or absorb it into scale.

Use only a few hierarchical parameters, such as:

- click noise;
- depth-noise magnitude;
- temporal correlation;
- contamination probability for small/edge-sensitive objects.

One scalar prior cannot identify all four per scene.

### Cheap falsification test

Before implementing the full hierarchy, test whether your label/physical-estimate residuals vary in the predicted directions with:

1. \(1/v\);
2. radial versus transverse motion;
3. object pixel diameter;
4. proximity of the click region to a depth discontinuity proxy.

If these patterns do not appear out of sample, another noise emulator is unlikely to help.

Fit noise with a likelihood or predictive-distribution criterion, and then choose the MRA-optimal action. Directly tuning a different MRA multiplier for each tiny subgroup will overfit.

## Things I would challenge in your current interpretation

### 1. “Lab sizes are .94× because of motion blur” is not established

Blur commonly **extends** a silhouette in the motion direction. Thresholding can shrink it, but a nearly uniform 6% deficit also suggests:

- visible-surface depth instead of object-center depth;
- an intrinsics/resize convention;
- dimension-definition mismatch;
- systematic segmentation erosion.

For a ball, center-pixel depth is approximately the front surface. Using \(z_{\rm surface}\) where \(z_{\rm center}\) is needed gives an approximately \(R/z_{\rm center}\) relative size deficit.

Check static/sharp versus blurred frames, and deficit versus \(R/z\). This is a cheap diagnostic with potentially broad coverage.

### 2. Two depth samples do not determine instantaneous 3D speed or acceleration

Linear depth interpolation is an assumption about motion, not data recovery. For acceleration, it directly removes radial acceleration. Better focal calibration will not repair that.

### 3. Geometric agreement with the calibration prior is tautological

Validation must involve another fact, another depth, another time interval, or withheld image measurements. A zero prior residual is not a useful quality feature.

### 4. More stable LLM answers are not necessarily more accurate

Your three-to-seven-run saturation is consistent with shared bias. Similarly, several monocular geometry models can agree because they share natural-image and ordinary-FOV priors.

### 5. “Balls read too far” is not a generic ToF consequence

A correct foreground center pixel usually reads the **front surface**, which is nearer than the center. Reading too far requires an additional mechanism: background contamination, holes, registration error, or multipath. Those imply different size and motion corrections.

### 6. Your lab motion ceiling may be especially low for acceleration

If acceleration is a raw second difference, independent 1 cm position noise contributes
\[
25^2\sqrt6\,(.01)\approx15.3\ {\rm m/s^2}
\]
standard deviation in that coordinate. That is already larger than gravity.

If observed acceleration labels are much more stable, that is evidence against an unsmoothed independent-noise interpretation—not a reason to increase the correction factor.
