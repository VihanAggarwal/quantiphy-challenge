Strategic Imperatives for the NeurIPS 2026 QuantiPhy Challenge: Overcoming 3D Geometry and Label Noise Bottlenecks
Executive Summary and Benchmark Context
The NeurIPS 2026 QuantiPhy Challenge represents a paradigm shift in the evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), demanding an evolution from qualitative physical plausibility to strict, numerically grounded kinematic inference. The benchmark rigorously tests a model's capacity to infer exact physical quantities—specifically size, velocity, and acceleration—from video clips paired with a single, exactly true physical prior. The dataset encompasses 3,355 video-question pairs derived from 569 unique videos, stratified across simulated, laboratory, and in-the-wild environments. To evaluate these predictions, the challenge employs Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA), a threshold-averaged metric that rewards approximate correctness while penalizing relative scale errors across confidence thresholds ranging from 0.10 to 0.95.
Currently, human annotators achieve an average MRA of 55.6, while leading proprietary models such as ChatGPT-5.1 reach 53.1, and open-weight models like Qwen3-VL-Instruct-32B achieve 46.0. The systemic bottleneck for advanced automated pipelines, as evidenced by a performance plateau at an MRA of approximately 0.81 against a leaderboard ceiling of 0.8525, is localized almost entirely within the three-dimensional reasoning categories (S3 for static size in 3D, and D3 for dynamics in 3D). The existing hybrid architecture utilizes a VLM (Claude Opus) to extract coarse two-dimensional object tracks, which are subsequently passed to a classical pinhole geometry solver alongside a stated depth prior. While effective for planar 2D motion, this architecture suffers catastrophic degradation when applied to 3D simulation renders with unknown camera intrinsics, as well as laboratory captures afflicted by systemic annotation noise.
The following analysis delivers an exhaustive diagnostic breakdown of the dominant failure modes in these two critical subsets. It subsequently proposes a strictly ranked sequence of eight novel, mathematically grounded methodologies that have not yet been deployed in the existing pipeline. These interventions focus on the integration of state-of-the-art foundation geometry models, noise-robust kinematic differentiation, probabilistic MRA optimization, and analytical bias correction.
Diagnostic Analysis of Dominant Errors and Realistic Ceilings
To engineer a solution capable of breaching the current leaderboard ceiling, it is necessary to formally diagnose the mathematical and systemic failures occurring within the two most problematic data subsets: the 3D Simulation clips and the 3D Laboratory clips.

Critique of Current Reasoning and Failed Methodologies
The provided documentation details several optimization attempts that yielded no improvement, including Large Language Model (LLM) ensembling, optical flow with GrabCut tracking, zoomed object measurement, and the application of uniform speed factors. The failure of these specific methods is highly diagnostic, revealing systemic flaws in the underlying assumptions of the current pipeline that must be rectified.
The failure of LLM ensembling, wherein GPT and Gemini were introduced as extra voters without yielding any ensemble gain, indicates a high degree of correlation in the failure modes of modern multi-modal architectures. Vision-Language Models share similar training data distributions, tokenization architectures, and optimization objectives. While they possess a qualitative, generalized physical intuition, they entirely lack sub-pixel spatial grounding. Attempting to extract precise two-dimensional tracks or direct numerical kinematic answers from these models will inherently saturate quickly, as their bounding box and point localizations are fundamentally constrained by the resolution of their vision encoders, which downsample visual inputs into coarse spatial patches.
Zooming in on object extents to re-measure size failed because the error in three-dimensional size estimation is not primarily a function of two-dimensional pixel resolution. It is a function of the perspective ambiguity inherent to projective geometry and the aforementioned spatial foreshortening. Extracting high-resolution bounding boxes of an object viewed at an oblique angle will still yield an incorrect metric size when algebraically treated as fronto-parallel.
The failure of dense optical flow and GrabCut methodologies is attributable to the fact that optical flow computes dense pixel correspondences indiscriminately. It does not differentiate between the target object's rigid translation and the apparent motion caused by camera rotation, focal length shifts, object articulation, or background occlusion. Integrating dense optical flow without robust kinematic outlier rejection incorporates background motion and articulation noise directly into the target's trajectory.
The application of a uniform multiplier, such as uniformly scaling laboratory speeds by a factor of 1.40, failed because the annotator bias is not a scalar multiplier; it is an additive, magnitude-dependent bias. As established by the Rician distribution of noisy displacement magnitudes, the percentage error is inversely proportional to the true speed of the object. Fast-moving objects require almost no correction, while slow-moving objects require a massive correction. A uniform scale factor over-corrects fast objects, destroying their MRA scores, while simultaneously under-correcting slow objects.
Specific Misconceptions in Pipeline Logic
The assertion that "The LLM's direct guesses are better than geometry on 3D non-lab" is a mischaracterization of the failure mode. The geometric principles are not inherently worse; rather, the inputs provided to the geometry solver are profoundly corrupted. By utilizing an LLM to generate pixel-level bounding boxes and point tracks, the pipeline injects massive quantization noise into a highly sensitive algebraic solver. The LLM's direct numerical guesses only appear superior because they rely on generalized, pre-trained semantic priors (e.g., matching the phrase "white car" to a standard wheelbase of 2.8 meters). While this brute-force semantic retrieval functions adequately for standardized objects, it completely collapses on non-standard simulation assets or highly specific dynamic velocity queries.
The observation that focal length is poorly conditioned when the prior moves along the line of sight is mathematically correct, but the pipeline's response—attempting to solve for the focal length dynamically on a per-clip basis using object motion—is fundamentally misguided. Focal length is an intrinsic property of the camera, entirely independent of the target object's trajectory. It must be predicted globally from the entire image context, utilizing stationary scene features rather than relying on the noisy trajectory of a single moving subject.
Finally, attempting to emulate a backward-difference label convention to match annotator behavior introduces temporal aliasing. The annotators utilized forward differences. Emulating backward differences introduces a strict temporal phase shift in the velocity curve. At 24 frames per second, a one-frame phase shift during a rapid acceleration phase results in massive instantaneous velocity errors. The focus must remain on predicting the true continuous velocity curve, and then evaluating it explicitly at the requested timestamp.
Ranked Methodological Interventions
The following eight methods are strictly concrete, mathematically robust, and previously untried within the stated pipeline. They are ranked by their expected MRA gain relative to implementation effort and computational cost. Each method includes a specific, low-cost validation strategy leveraging the available 159 validation labels and 85 stated family facts.

Rather than relying on an algebraic pinhole solver that collapses due to noisy tracks and missing depth priors, the pipeline must leverage recent breakthroughs in monocular metric depth estimation. Specifically, it must employ models that explicitly decouple and predict the camera's focal length from single-image features.

Methodology:
Deploy Apple's DepthPro directly on the simulation frames. DepthPro utilizes a patch-based multi-scale Vision Transformer operating at a fixed high resolution of 1536x1536. It produces exceptionally sharp metric depth maps while explicitly predicting the absolute focal length in pixels via a separate, dedicated self-attention head. The model outputs a canonical inverse depth map that is transformed into a highly accurate metric scale without requiring any EXIF metadata.
Alternatively, Microsoft's MoGe predicts an affine-invariant three-dimensional point map alongside a specialized mask that excludes regions with undefined geometry, such as the sky. MoGe utilizes a Robust, Optimal, and Efficient (ROE) solver to recover the camera focal length and Z-axis shift directly from the predicted three-dimensional topology.
By passing a single frame of the simulation video to DepthPro or MoGe, the pipeline can extract a highly reliable focal length parameter. This predicted intrinsic parameter can then be hard-coded into the existing geometric solver, entirely bypassing the need to dynamically condition the focal length on the noisy object prior. Once the focal length is fixed, the depth prior provided in the text can be used to accurately rescale the resulting affine point map or relative depth map into a strict, numerically accurate metric space.

Since the focal length in standard Blender renders is static, a low variance in the predicted focal length across frames immediately validates the model's stability. Subsequently, compare the extracted focal length against the scene families where the focal length might be implicitly derivable via algebraic cross-referencing.
Rank 2: Noise-Robust Kinematic Differentiation via Savitzky-Golay Filtering
The computation of velocity and acceleration from sparse, noisy trajectories using forward or backward finite differences acts as a mathematical high-pass filter, catastrophically amplifying high-frequency localization noise. The human annotations in the laboratory dataset suffer from this amplification, but the computational pipeline's internal predictions must be mathematically smoothed.
Methodology:
To accurately estimate the kinematics of the target object from the video frames, the pipeline must replace discrete difference equations with a Savitzky-Golay (S-G) smoothing filter. The S-G filter operates by fitting a low-degree polynomial to a sliding window of sequential spatial points utilizing linear least squares optimization.

Apply the Savitzky-Golay filter to the raw, noisy ground-truth tracks generated by the existing laboratory label emulator. Measure the statistical variance in the predicted velocity over time. Rank 3: Dense Mask-Centroid Tracking via SAM 2 and CoTracker3
The primary Vision-Language Model (Claude Opus) currently generates tracks with localization errors ranging from a few pixels to tens of pixels. In projective three-dimensional space, a ten-pixel localization error on a small, distant object equates to multiple meters of geometric error, entirely destroying kinematic accuracy.
Methodology:
The VLM must be removed from the temporal tracking pipeline entirely. Instead, utilize the VLM solely to output a single referring text coordinate (e.g., "click on the blue car") for the first frame of the video. Pass this specific coordinate into the Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2) to generate a precise, sub-pixel segmentation mask of the target object.
Following segmentation, deploy CoTracker3, a state-of-the-art transformer-based tracking model capable of tracking a quasi-dense grid of points through severe occlusions. CoTracker3 utilizes proxy tokens and a sliding window architecture to manage computational complexity while maintaining temporal consistency. Sample a dense grid of points strictly within the bounds of the SAM 2 mask on the initial frame, and employ CoTracker3's offline mode to track this ensemble of points across the entire temporal extent of the video.
Rather than relying on the noisy trajectory of a single pixel coordinate, compute the spatial centroid of the tracked points at each individual frame. By averaging the independent trajectories of hundreds of points belonging exclusively to the target object, the zero-mean independent localization noise cancels out. This yields a hyper-smooth, highly accurate trajectory that perfectly interfaces with the Savitzky-Golay filter and the pinhole geometry solver.
Cheap Validation Strategy:
Execute CoTracker3 on static background elements, such as the ground plane or laboratory walls. Because the camera in the laboratory setup is completely fixed, the background tracks should exhibit exactly zero velocity. The magnitude of the apparent background track motion provides a direct, computationally cheap measurement of the sub-pixel noise floor inherent to the tracking algorithm.

Rank 5: Global Scene Geometry Conditioning via VGGT
When local geometry solvers fail due to edge-case perspective foreshortening and missing depth priors on the specific target object, a global geometric inference model can provide robust scene-level priors.
Methodology:
Integrate the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT), a feed-forward neural network introduced in 2025. Unlike DepthPro, which operates purely on single static images, VGGT is specifically designed to infer comprehensive three-dimensional attributes from multi-view or sequential video frames. VGGT alternates between frame-wise and global self-attention layers, effectively outputting both intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters, as well as dense, globally coherent point clouds.
By feeding two to four keyframes of the simulation video into VGGT, the model outputs a coherent three-dimensional scene structure. While the baseline scale of this structure is relative, the text-provided depth prior can be utilized to compute a single, global scalar multiplier. This multiplier is then applied to the entire VGGT point cloud, instantly resolving the physical sizes and spatial distances of all objects within the scene, completely circumventing the need to rely on error-prone two-dimensional bounding box measurements.
Cheap Validation Strategy:
Extract the three-dimensional size of a known "prior" object directly from the VGGT point cloud, scale it utilizing the provided depth prior, and compare it to the exactly known ground truth size derived from the scene families dataset. If VGGT consistently preserves the aspect ratios and relative scales of objects across different depths with higher fidelity than the classical pinhole solver, it represents a superior geometric foundation.

Rank 8: Label-Free Equivariance Training (EquiSD) for VLM Priors
The underlying Vision-Language Models exhibit exceptionally poor scale equivariance. For instance, testing reveals that the Qwen3-VL-8B model possesses a median slope of only 0.14 when scaling predictions based on camera distance, indicating a catastrophic failure to adjust predictions relative to changing depth priors.
Methodology:
Implement EquiSD, a label-free equivariance training methodology specifically designed to improve scale equivariance in VLMs. EquiSD requires only one query per training question and utilizes no manual annotations. By enforcing consistency across varied input scales during an offline fine-tuning or adaptation phase, the VLM can be trained to dynamically adjust its internal priors based on the provided depth information. This directly attacks the core failure mode of the VLM, forcing its semantic priors to behave in a geometrically consistent manner.
Cheap Validation Strategy:
Apply the EquiSD adaptation to a small subset of the open-source VLM weights and evaluate the scale response slope on the 90 QuantiPhy validation questions that supply camera distances. An increase in the response slope from the baseline 0.14 toward the ideal 1.0 indicates that the model is successfully learning physical scale equivariance.
Execution and Integration Roadmap
To operationalize these methodologies within the stringent ten-day remaining timeline, utilizing the available 96 GB GPU architecture, a structured integration roadmap is required.
The initial phase must prioritize the overhaul of simulation intrinsics. The pipeline should load DepthPro and MoGe, passing the middle frame of every 3D Simulation video through the models to extract the  focallength_px
 and affine FOV parameters. These extracted intrinsics must immediately replace the dynamic focal length variables within the existing pinhole solver. Simultaneously, the pipeline must discard bounding-box extents in favor of utilizing DepthPro's metric depth map to compute the true Euclidean distance between the three-dimensional coordinates of the target object's spatial boundaries, inherently resolving all perspective foreshortening errors.
Following the geometry overhaul, the focus must shift to laboratory tracking and kinematics. The Claude Opus bounding boxes should be utilized solely to initialize SAM 2 on the first frame, generating a sub-pixel mask. A dense array of 100 points sampled within this mask must then be processed through the CoTracker3 offline model, leveraging the full 96 GB VRAM to ensure robust temporal tracking through any occlusions. The spatial centroid of these points at each frame constitutes the new, high-fidelity trajectory.
This trajectory must then be passed through the Savitzky-Golay filter, utilizing a polynomial window length that covers approximately 0.3 seconds of temporal data to account for the 24 frames-per-second jitter. The first derivative of this smoothed polynomial yields the true physical velocity. Finally, the true velocity must be passed through the Rician bias injection algorithm, calculating the anticipated annotator noise (

In the final days of the challenge, the pipeline should integrate the log-normal MRA surrogate loss optimizer to aggregate all ensemble predictions. The existing scene-family logic should remain active, but the family-derived scale corrections must be applied strictly to the output of the new foundation models, entirely replacing the raw, geometrically flawed LLM outputs.
Conclusion
The 0.81 MRA barrier currently stalling progress in the QuantiPhy Challenge is not an insurmountable limit of modern visual reasoning; it is a direct artifact of utilizing generalized, semantically driven Vision-Language Models for tasks that demand strict metric photogrammetry. By transitioning intrinsic recovery to foundation models like DepthPro and MoGe, offloading temporal tracking to the sub-pixel precision of CoTracker3, mathematically smoothing kinematics via Savitzky-Golay filtering, and analytically modeling the systemic human annotation noise via Rician distributions, the pipeline can effectively neutralize both the geometry and label-bias bottlenecks. The rigorous execution of these untried, computationally feasible methodologies provides a clear, quantitatively sound trajectory to surpass the current 0.8525 leaderboard ceiling and achieve state-of-the-art performance in kinematic inference.
